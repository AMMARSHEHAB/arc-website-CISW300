Fluffy Pancakes

An easy start to a slow morning

A bowl, a whisk and a warm pan are all you need for this breakfast. Keep the batter a little lumpy, give it a short rest and cook gently for a stack of soft, golden pancakes.

Makes about 8 medium pancakes · Serves 4
Preparation: 10 minutes · Cooking: about 15 minutes

Gather the ingredients · Make your first stack

Small things that help

  • Measure flour lightly
  • Use fresh baking powder
  • Avoid overmixing
  • Rest for 5 minutes
  • Choose medium-low heat
  • Flip just once

Into the mixing bowl

  • 1½ cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • ¼ teaspoon fine salt
  • 1¼ cups (300 ml) milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons (28 g) butter, melted and slightly cooled
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract, optional
  • A little butter or oil for the pan
  • Maple syrup and fresh fruit, to serve

From bowl to stack

  1. Start with the dry ingredients. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder and salt in a large bowl. Spoon flour into the measuring cup and level it instead of packing it down.
  2. Mix the wet ingredients. In a second bowl, whisk the milk, egg, cooled melted butter and optional vanilla until evenly combined.
  3. Bring the batter together. Pour the wet mixture into the dry mixture. Stir gently just until the dry flour disappears. A few small lumps are fine.
  4. Pause for 5 minutes. Let the batter rest while you set out plates and toppings. It should be thick but pourable; add a small splash of milk if needed.
  5. Cook the first side. Warm a nonstick pan over medium-low heat and grease it lightly. Pour about ¼ cup of batter for each pancake. Cook until bubbles appear and the edges begin to look set, usually 2–3 minutes.
  6. Flip once. Turn each pancake with a spatula and cook for another 1–2 minutes, until golden and cooked through. Lower the heat if the outside darkens before the middle cooks. Do not press the pancakes flat.
  7. Make a breakfast stack. Repeat with the remaining batter, lightly greasing the pan as needed. Serve warm with maple syrup, berries, banana or a spoon of yogurt.

Pancake tips

A gentle mix

Stirring less helps keep the pancakes tender. Stop when the flour has disappeared instead of trying to beat every lump out.


01 · Mixing

Watch the first one

The first pancake helps you judge the heat. If it browns too quickly, turn the heat down before pouring the next batch.


02 · Flipping

Leave room to flip

Small pancakes are easier to turn. Leave space between them and wait for bubbles and set edges before reaching for the spatula.
